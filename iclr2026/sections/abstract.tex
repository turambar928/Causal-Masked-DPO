Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) treats an entire rejected response as negative evidence. In multi-step reasoning, however, a rejected solution often contains a correct prefix followed by a localized first error and downstream steps conditioned on that error. Penalizing the full trajectory can therefore suppress useful reasoning behavior. We propose Causal-Masked DPO (\cmdpo), a lightweight modification of DPO that applies step-level causal weights only to the rejected response. Given an estimated first erroneous step, \cmdpo masks the correct prefix, assigns full penalty to the first error, and decays the penalty on later steps. This preserves the trajectory-level preference-pair format and does not require constructing step-level preference pairs or training a process reward model. On controlled arithmetic preference experiments with Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and LoRA, vanilla DPO consistently harms held-out generation accuracy, while \cmdpo is the strongest method on average across three seeds. The current evidence supports \cmdpo as a promising mechanism for reducing over-penalty in reasoning alignment, while also showing that its margin over first-error-only training remains small and requires broader validation.
