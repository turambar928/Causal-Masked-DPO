\section{Results}

\subsection{Single-Seed Scaling}

Table~\ref{tab:single-seed} summarizes the strongest single-seed held-out result from the harder2000 setting. Vanilla DPO substantially hurts generation accuracy, dropping from the base model's 41.0\% to 18.6\%. In contrast, \cmdpo reaches 49.0\%, outperforming prefix-masked DPO and first-error-only DPO.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Held-out harder arithmetic accuracy for the harder2000 single-seed run.}
\label{tab:single-seed}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Model & Accuracy & Correct & Total \\
\midrule
Base & 0.410 & 205 & 500 \\
Vanilla DPO & 0.186 & 93 & 500 \\
Prefix-masked DPO & 0.360 & 180 & 500 \\
First-error-only DPO & 0.440 & 220 & 500 \\
\cmdpo{} & \textbf{0.490} & \textbf{245} & 500 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{table}

Likelihood decomposition in this setting is consistent with the proposed mechanism: vanilla DPO strongly suppresses correct prefixes, while masked variants preserve them. \cmdpo also applies more pressure to the first-error region than first-error-only in the measured decomposition, while retaining a decayed treatment of post-error suffix tokens.

\subsection{Multi-Seed Replication}

Because the single-seed margin could overstate the effect, we replicated harder2000 training with seeds 1, 2, and 3. Table~\ref{tab:multiseed-full} reports the full held-out generation results. Vanilla DPO is harmful in every seed. Prefix masking reduces the damage but remains below the base model. First-error-only and \cmdpo both improve over the base model on average.

Table~\ref{tab:multiseed-agg} shows that \cmdpo is directionally strongest on average, but the margin over first-error-only is only 0.67 percentage points. This is an important qualification: current generation-accuracy evidence strongly rejects vanilla DPO in this setting, supports causal masking over uniform rejected penalties, but does not yet establish a large advantage of suffix decay over first-error-only training.

\subsection{Interpretation}

The experiments support three preliminary conclusions. First, full rejected-trajectory penalization is a poor fit for partially correct reasoning traces: vanilla DPO repeatedly degrades generation accuracy. Second, protecting the correct prefix is necessary but insufficient; prefix-masked DPO is consistently better than vanilla but still below the base model in the three-seed run. Third, \cmdpo gives the best average result, suggesting that decayed post-error penalty may help, but the current margin requires larger evaluations and more realistic reasoning benchmarks.
